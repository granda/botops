# Four layers

Matthew talks to one Grok Bot. The rest of the fleet — household, work, writing, research, the one-shot temps that browse a site or launch a Cursor cloud agent — does not get his DMs. They get tickets. He gets a link when something needs a decision.

| Layer | Who | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Me | Operator | Talk to Chief of Staff. Review tickets. Delete finished temps. |
| L1 | Chief of Staff | Intake. Tickets. Assign an L2. Surface links. |
| L2 | Household, Work, Notion, Writer, … | Own a domain. Spawn L3s. Open swarms. Update Notion from handbacks. |
| L3 | One-shot temps + cadence runners | Do the work. Browse, research, launch Cursor agents, fire routines. |

He does not message L2 or L3 unless he chooses to. The point of the stack is that he usually does not have to.

## L1 — Chief of Staff

Chief of Staff is the only bot treated as a colleague. Intake lands here. It makes sure a Project and a Task exist, assigns an Owner L2, DMs that L2, and later drops the ticket link.

CoS does not browse, drive a desktop, launch Cursor cloud agents, spawn L3s, or own routines. If it starts doing IC work, the chat stops being decision-dense.

## L2 — domain owners

Household, Work, Notion, Writer, and whatever durable L2s Matthew has approved. Each owns a domain. They spawn L3s (including cadence runners), open CreateChannel swarms when a job needs more than one L3, sit quietly in that swarm, and manage Notion from the handbacks.

They DM CoS when Matthew should look. They do not talk to him, run Cursor cloud agents, or hold a standing routine on their own chat. A new durable L2 only exists after he says yes.

## L3 — temps and cadence runners

This is the IC layer. One-shot temps and cadence runners browse, use the desktop, research, create Tasks rows, spawn more L3s, launch and monitor Cursor cloud agents, dedupe overlapping swarm work, and own every recurring routine.

They do not message Matthew. They do not create durable L2s.

If it is recurring, it is an L3 cadence runner. If it needs a browser, a desktop, or a Cursor cloud agent, it is L3.

## Notion is the source of record

Everything important lives on Projects and Tasks. Work is handed by ticket URL, not a paste of the last forty messages.

Status moves in one direction unless something actually changed:

Backlog → Ready → In progress → Waiting / Blocked → In review → Done / Cancelled

- **Waiting** means someone outside this stack.
- **Blocked** means Matthew — and Blocked requires a Block reason.
- Visual work that lands in **In review** needs scrollable pictures attached to the ticket. If he has to reconstruct what the bot saw from prose, the handback failed.

He reviews Notion. He does not reconstruct jobs from chat.
